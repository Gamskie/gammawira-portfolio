MSC shipment doc & booking pipeline

A small Python pipeline I built for my own export-ops work at an Indonesian commodity briquette exporter. This page is a quick honest tour of what's wired up, what's still manual, and the trade-offs.

scope: one carrier (MSC) tested on real bookings: TR2601, CTMD6 submit step: still manual

The problem I had

Booking a container shipment at the day job took the same handful of steps every time. Sales would drop the IPL (Instruction Packing List) Excel into a folder. From there I'd compute Packing / Cooling / Production dates around Indonesian holidays, fill out five Word documents (SI, Weathering Report, Process Statement, LOI, Intermodal Booking Form), convert them to PDF, draft the booking email to the right MSC region contact, and submit through MyMSC. Once the booking went through, I'd paste the booking number back into the IPL.

None of those steps were hard. They were just tedious, easy to mistype, and added up to an afternoon I didn't enjoy.

The pipeline, end to end

build_shipment_data.py    ← orchestrator
  ↓ produces shipment_data.json
  ├─→ generate_docs.py        fills 5 Word templates
  ├─→ convert_to_pdf.ps1      Word → PDF (Word COM)
  ├─→ update_ipl_booking.py   writes booking# back to IPL Excel
  ├─→ save_ipl_pdf.ps1        IPL Excel → PDF (Excel COM)
  └─→ compose_email.py        drafts TO/CC/Subject/Body

What's actually wired up

  • IPL parser — reads the active invoice sheet, pulls consignee, POD, container, weights, cartons, invoice number, product type. Tolerates a couple of common sheet-name variants.
  • Date engine — Indonesian-public-holiday-aware. Given a stuffing date and product type, it shifts back to give Packing / Cooling / Production windows. Supports a shift_days offset for multi-booking weeks.
  • Doc generator — fills five Word templates by find-and-replace. PowerShell + Word COM converts to PDF for round-trip fidelity.
  • Email draft composer — picks the right MSC region contact (POD keyword first, then code prefix as fallback) and renders subject / body from a JSON config. This part is template-based, not LLM-generated.
  • IPL booking writer — pastes the booking number into the right cell across all the relevant sheets, with a .bak backup.

What's still manual

  • Submitting to MyMSC. Once the docs and email draft are ready, I still log into the portal and submit by hand. Playwright automation for MyMSC is on the to-do list.
  • DG (Dangerous Goods) form handling. Old .doc format that needs a LibreOffice convert step I haven't wired in yet.
  • A few template auto-detections that currently rely on the previous booking's filled values being available — fine in steady state, fragile when the template changes.

Trade-offs along the way

Template fill vs. LLM-generated email

I started writing the email composer as a Claude prompt. Then I noticed the email is the same five lines every time, just with different dates and POD. A JSON config with region routing solved it deterministically, with zero API cost and no risk of an LLM nudging field values around. There's a version of this pipeline where Claude does the email — it just isn't this one.

Word COM on Windows vs. cross-platform PDF

The team needs the rendered PDF to look exactly like the existing Word templates — same fonts, same spacing, same logo placement. python-docx alone wasn't enough; I drove Word and Excel through PowerShell + COM to round-trip perfectly. That ties the pipeline to my Windows machine, which is fine for now.

Tested on two shipments, not a hundred

The pipeline produces correct outputs on TR2601 (Istanbul, EU region) and CTMD6 (Rotterdam, EU region). I haven't run it across the full 2026 history yet. If you're a prospect reading this and wondering whether I'd over-claim a number — I'd rather show you the two real test cases than invent a "20+ hours/week saved" headline.

What I'd do for someone else

The pattern is general: parse a templated input, do the deterministic data work, fill outbound documents, draft the outbound communication, leave the irreversible action (the actual portal submit, the actual email send) for a human to confirm. If your team has a workflow that fits — Excel + email + a vendor portal, repeated weekly — I can usually scope something similar in a 30-minute call.
